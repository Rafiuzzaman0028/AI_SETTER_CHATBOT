Assessment 
The bot did well here: 
It introduced the free discovery call first before starting the fit questions. 
It explained why it needs to ask prequalification questions. 
The flow felt more natural compared to the earlier version. 
But these need to be fixed before deployment: 
1. It asked the fitness question twice 
The bot asked: 
“Would you say you’re out of shape, average, or built?” 
Then after the lead answered “average,” it asked again: 
“How would you describe your current fitness/lifestyle right now...?” 
This feels repetitive and can make the lead feel like the bot did not understand the answer. 
2. The “health and wealth” wording is still too blunt 
Since Jamie wants this included, we should keep the question, but the wording should be 
softer. 
Better version: 
Got it. And just to understand where you’re at overall, how would you describe your current 
f
itness and lifestyle right now: out of shape, average, or in good shape? 
3. The bot disqualified the lead too early 
The lead only answered: 
- US 
- 38 
- Single 
- Average fitness 
Then the bot said: 
“Private coaching doesn’t seem like it’s in the cards right now.” 
That feels too sudden. It should not disqualify the lead based only on those answers unless 
there are missing qualification rules we have not tested yet. 
4. The bot changed direction too fast 
It moved from “free discovery call” to “Online Dating Mastery” without clearly explaining why. 
That can feel confusing to the lead. 
5. Brand/persona issue 
The bot said: 
“Amanda can still help you” 
If this is Jamie Date’s chatbot, we need to confirm if “Amanda” is intentional. If not, that 
should be corrected because it creates brand confusion.
